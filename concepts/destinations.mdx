---
title: "Destinations"
description: "Learn how connected social accounts become publishing targets."
---

A **destination** is a connected social account that can receive content. It combines a provider, an account identity, connection status, and publishing capabilities.

Examples include a LinkedIn profile, a Mastodon account, or a Slack channel.

## Connection lifecycle

1. Create a connection session for a provider.
2. Send the person connecting the account to the returned authorization URL.
3. The provider redirects back to OpenPMM.
4. OpenPMM creates or updates the destination.
5. List destinations until the connected account appears.

OpenPMM keeps provider access tokens on the server. Your integration receives destination IDs, not provider credentials.

## Enabled and default destinations

An enabled destination can receive new publishing work. A workspace may mark a destination as its default, but integrations should still send an explicit `destination_id` when the target matters.

<Tip>
  Store destination IDs in your own configuration. Display the provider and account label to people, but send the stable ID to the API.
</Tip>
